# Lab 07 - 5S, Standard Work, and Visual Controls

## Objectives

- Apply 5S thinking.
- Identify visual control opportunities.
- Draft simple standard work.
- Support sustainment.

## Scenario

Small improvements are easier to sustain when the workplace is organized, the standard is clear, and the team can see when something is not normal.

## Steps

1. Choose one physical workspace, digital folder, form, queue, or process step.
2. Apply Sort: list what is needed and not needed.
3. Apply Set in Order: decide where needed items or information should be located.
4. Apply Shine: identify what should be cleaned, corrected, clarified, or updated.
5. Apply Standardize: define what good looks like.
6. Apply Sustain: define how the standard will be checked.
7. Identify where a label, checklist, color code, status board, or template would help.
8. Draft a short standard work instruction.
9. Include task name, steps, expected result, and check method.
10. Ask a partner to review whether the instruction is clear.
11. Revise the standard work based on feedback.

## Validation

- The 5S actions are practical.
- The visual control makes abnormal conditions easier to see.
- Standard work is written simply.
- Another learner can follow the instruction.

## Deliverables

- 5S action list.
- Visual control idea.
- Draft standard work.
- Feedback notes.

## Review Questions

1. What does 5S help prevent?
2. Why are visual controls useful?
3. What makes standard work easy to follow?
4. How does sustainment differ from a one-time cleanup?
