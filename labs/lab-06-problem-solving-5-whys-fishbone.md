# Lab 06 - Problem Solving with 5 Whys and Fishbone

## Objectives

- Write a fact-based problem statement.
- Use 5 Whys.
- Build a Fishbone diagram.
- Identify evidence needed before action.

## Scenario

The team has identified a visible issue. Before suggesting solutions, you will explore possible causes in a structured and respectful way.

## Steps

1. Select the issue from Lab 05.
2. Write a problem statement using facts from the process or data.
3. Ask why the problem happens.
4. Continue asking why until you reach a process-related possible cause.
5. Avoid blaming a person as the final cause.
6. Create a Fishbone diagram.
7. Use categories such as people, process, tools, materials, measurement, and environment.
8. Add possible causes to each category.
9. Circle two causes that seem most likely.
10. Write what evidence would support or reject each cause.
11. Identify who can help confirm the cause.
12. Write one safe next investigation step.

## Validation

- The problem statement is factual.
- The 5 Whys chain does not stop at blame.
- Fishbone causes are plausible.
- Evidence needed is clearly listed.

## Deliverables

- Problem statement.
- 5 Whys worksheet.
- Fishbone diagram.
- Evidence-needed list.
- Next investigation step.

## Review Questions

1. Why should teams avoid jumping straight to solutions?
2. Why is blame weak root cause analysis?
3. How does Fishbone thinking help a team?
4. What is the difference between a possible cause and a proven cause?
