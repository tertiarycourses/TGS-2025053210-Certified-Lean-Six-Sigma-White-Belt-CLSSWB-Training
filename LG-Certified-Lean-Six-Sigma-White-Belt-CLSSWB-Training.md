# Learner Guide - Certified Lean Six Sigma White Belt (CLSSWB) Training

## Course Overview

This learner guide supports a practical Certified Lean Six Sigma White Belt course. It is designed for learners who are new to Lean Six Sigma and want to understand how to recognize waste, support improvement projects, and contribute to a culture of continuous improvement.

White Belts are not expected to lead complex projects. Their value is in understanding the language of improvement, observing work carefully, helping collect basic data, sharing process knowledge, and supporting Green Belts, Black Belts, managers, and process owners.

## Core Ideas

### Lean

Lean focuses on increasing customer value by reducing waste and improving flow.

### Six Sigma

Six Sigma focuses on reducing defects and variation by using data and structured problem solving.

### DMAIC

DMAIC is the improvement roadmap:

1. Define the problem and customer need.
2. Measure current performance.
3. Analyze possible causes.
4. Improve the process.
5. Control the improved process.

### White Belt Role

A White Belt can:

- Recognize improvement opportunities.
- Explain basic Lean Six Sigma terms.
- Participate in improvement discussions.
- Help map processes.
- Help collect simple data.
- Identify waste in daily work.
- Support implementation of standard work and visual controls.

## Lab 01 Guide - Lean Six Sigma Foundations

### Objectives

- Understand Lean and Six Sigma at an introductory level.
- Explain DMAIC.
- Understand the White Belt support role.
- Identify examples of improvement in daily work.

### Steps

1. Write your own definition of Lean.
2. Write your own definition of Six Sigma.
3. List the five DMAIC phases.
4. For each DMAIC phase, write one simple activity that happens in that phase.
5. List three things a White Belt can do in an improvement project.
6. List two things a White Belt should escalate to a Green Belt, Black Belt, manager, or process owner.
7. Choose one process from your work or daily life.
8. Write one sentence explaining why that process matters.
9. Write one problem you have seen in that process.
10. Share your example with a partner.

### Deliverables

- Lean and Six Sigma definitions.
- DMAIC summary.
- White Belt role notes.
- Selected process example.

### Checkpoint

You can explain Lean Six Sigma to a colleague without using technical jargon.

## Lab 02 Guide - Customer Value and Process Thinking

### Objectives

- Identify the customer of a process.
- Separate value-added and non-value-added work.
- Define simple process boundaries.
- Build a basic SIPOC.

### Steps

1. Select the process you will use for the course.
2. Identify the customer or receiver of the process output.
3. Write what the customer needs from the process.
4. Identify the process start point and end point.
5. List suppliers, inputs, process steps, outputs, and customers.
6. Create a simple SIPOC table.
7. List five activities in the process.
8. Mark each activity as value-added, business-required, or non-value-added.
9. Circle one step that seems slow, confusing, repeated, or error-prone.
10. Add notes to your improvement journal.

### Deliverables

- Customer value worksheet.
- Simple SIPOC.
- Value-added activity list.
- Improvement opportunity note.

### Checkpoint

You can explain who the customer is and what part of the process creates value for them.

## Lab 03 Guide - Process Walk and Simple Map

### Objectives

- Observe the process where work happens.
- Draw a simple process map.
- Identify handoffs, queues, and waiting.
- Practise fact-based observation.

### Steps

1. Go to the place where the process happens, or review the workflow with someone who performs it.
2. Observe without blaming or correcting people.
3. Record the actual steps in order.
4. Note who performs each step.
5. Note handoffs between people, teams, tools, or systems.
6. Note waiting time, rework, repeated checking, or unclear decisions.
7. Draw a simple process map with boxes and arrows.
8. Add decision diamonds where the flow branches.
9. Mark one step that should be discussed with the process owner.
10. Share the map with a peer and ask whether it is understandable.

### Deliverables

- Process walk notes.
- Simple process map.
- Handoff and waiting notes.
- Process owner discussion point.

### Checkpoint

You can describe the process as it actually happens, not only as people think it happens.

## Lab 04 Guide - Identify TIMWOODS Waste

### Objectives

- Learn the eight wastes.
- Identify waste in a real process.
- Record observations without blame.
- Prioritize one waste area for improvement.

### Steps

1. Review the eight wastes: transport, inventory, motion, waiting, overproduction, overprocessing, defects, and skills.
2. Open the process map from Lab 03.
3. Review each process step.
4. Mark any transport waste.
5. Mark any inventory or work-in-progress waste.
6. Mark any motion waste.
7. Mark waiting time.
8. Mark overproduction or doing work before it is needed.
9. Mark overprocessing or extra checking.
10. Mark defects, errors, rework, or missing information.
11. Mark underused skills or missed ideas from people doing the work.
12. Choose the most important waste area to discuss.

### Deliverables

- TIMWOODS waste table.
- Marked process map.
- Priority waste statement.
- One improvement question.

### Checkpoint

You can point to a specific process step and explain what waste is present.

## Lab 05 Guide - Basic Data and Visuals

### Objectives

- Collect simple process data.
- Use a check sheet.
- Create a simple chart.
- Identify the largest issue category.

### Steps

1. Select one process issue from Lab 04.
2. Decide what can be counted safely and simply.
3. Create a check sheet with clear categories.
4. Collect sample data or use a trainer-provided dataset.
5. Count the number of occurrences in each category.
6. Create a simple bar chart.
7. Sort categories from largest to smallest.
8. Create a Pareto-style chart if your tool supports it.
9. If time data is available, create a run chart over time.
10. Write one sentence describing what the chart shows.
11. Write one question the data raises.

### Deliverables

- Check sheet.
- Tally table.
- Bar chart or Pareto-style chart.
- Run chart if time data is available.
- Data observation note.

### Checkpoint

You can use simple data to support a discussion about where to focus first.

## Lab 06 Guide - Problem Solving with 5 Whys and Fishbone

### Objectives

- Write a clear problem statement.
- Use 5 Whys.
- Build a simple Fishbone diagram.
- Separate possible causes from proven causes.

### Steps

1. Select the issue identified in Lab 05.
2. Write a problem statement using observable facts.
3. Ask why the issue happens.
4. Continue asking why until the cause becomes process-related.
5. Avoid blaming individuals.
6. Create a Fishbone diagram.
7. Use categories such as people, process, tools, materials, measurement, and environment.
8. Add possible causes under each category.
9. Circle causes that need more evidence.
10. Identify who could help confirm the cause.
11. Write one safe next step for investigation.

### Deliverables

- Problem statement.
- 5 Whys worksheet.
- Fishbone diagram.
- Evidence-needed list.
- Next investigation step.

### Checkpoint

You can discuss causes respectfully and avoid jumping straight to solutions.

## Lab 07 Guide - 5S, Standard Work, and Visual Controls

### Objectives

- Understand 5S.
- Identify visual control opportunities.
- Draft simple standard work.
- Support sustainable improvement habits.

### Steps

1. Choose one workspace, digital folder, form, queue, or process step.
2. Apply Sort: identify what is needed and not needed.
3. Apply Set in Order: decide where needed items or information should be placed.
4. Apply Shine: identify what should be cleaned, corrected, or clarified.
5. Apply Standardize: define a simple standard for the improved state.
6. Apply Sustain: define how the standard will be checked.
7. Identify where a label, checklist, status board, color code, or template would help.
8. Draft a one-page standard work instruction for a simple task.
9. Ask another learner whether the instruction is clear.
10. Revise the instruction based on feedback.

### Deliverables

- 5S action list.
- Visual control idea.
- Draft standard work.
- Feedback notes.

### Checkpoint

You can explain how 5S and standard work reduce confusion and variation.

## Lab 08 Guide - Improvement Summary and Action Plan

### Objectives

- Summarize the improvement opportunity.
- Define a small action plan.
- Identify support needed.
- Present the White Belt improvement story.

### Steps

1. Review all worksheets from Labs 01 to 07.
2. Write the process name and customer.
3. Summarize the problem in one sentence.
4. Summarize the waste or data observation.
5. Summarize the possible cause.
6. Write one practical improvement idea.
7. Identify the owner or team that should review the idea.
8. Define the first action, due date, and support needed.
9. Define how the team will know whether the change helped.
10. Prepare a three-minute presentation.
11. Present to a peer or trainer.
12. Record feedback and next steps.

### Deliverables

- Improvement summary.
- Action plan.
- Measurement idea.
- Presentation notes.
- Lessons learned.

### Checkpoint

You can communicate a simple improvement opportunity clearly, respectfully, and with evidence.
